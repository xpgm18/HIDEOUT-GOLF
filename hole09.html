Tips

Like most golf courses, and this one is no exception, holes #9 and #18 are “signature” holes. This is a fun hole with a slight dogleg right, followed by plenty of fairway, with a potentially not-so-easy approach shot to a 3-tiered green, blocked on the left by a tall stand of mature elm trees.

Long hitters might have fun taking a shortcut over the trees and ravine to the right of the fairway; However, if the ball doesn’t go far enough, a hidden 2nd clump of trees will eat your ball for lunch.

The rest of us should play the course as it lies. Placing the ball on top of that little crest or--likely--landing after the crest for a bouncy downhill roll is probably the best bet and very doable for many players.

Don't forget that all-important rule of golf: "if this is your last hole, you have to be satisfied with your drive!" If not, try again, but watch out you don't hold up any party behind you.

Fairway and Approach Shot Info

Careful, now. Don't get too giddy just because you had a good tee shot! You're itchin' to pull out that nice fairway wood and whack that ball straight towards the green, aren't you? That would be ill-advised: a tall stand of Elms blocks most or all of the green (you might get away with it if the flag is on the right side of the green). The better bet is to play the middle, or maybe even better, if the flag is on the left side of the green, the right part of the fairway. Either way, the fairway is mercifully wide. Careful though, long hitters could send the ball into the creek (hey, didn't we have enough of that creek on #8?) that traverses the fairway some 35 yards in front of the green. The green is a 3-tiered green, with the lowest tier on the right, so that might factor into your play.

Green Info

If your ball is on the same tier as the flag, you're in luck and it's straightforward; if not, you should have some fun making that pesky ball behave properly as it goes up or down a tier (or two!)
